Indications & Margin Principles
Removing the cancer with an oncologically adequate margin while preserving as much natural breast tissue and shape as possible.

Indications

  • Early-stage disease with a favourable tumour-to-breast size ratio, allowing a cosmetically acceptable result after excision.
  • Single, localised lesion (or a small number of lesions that can be excised through a shared approach) — not multicentric disease.
  • No contraindication to the mandatory adjuvant radiotherapy that accompanies breast-conserving surgery (e.g. prior chest radiotherapy, certain connective tissue diseases, pregnancy at the time treatment would be needed).

Margin status — the current standard

Disease typeMargin standard
Invasive carcinoma"No ink on tumour" — i.e. no tumour cells at the inked surgical margin, without a specified minimum distance
DCISA wider margin is generally sought (historically around 2mm), reflecting DCIS's tendency to be more extensive than imaging suggests
⚠️ Exam Trap

Don't apply the DCIS margin standard to invasive disease or vice versa — the "no ink on tumour" standard for invasive cancer reflects trial evidence that wider margins don't further reduce recurrence for invasive disease, whereas DCIS is managed more cautiously given its tendency to be more extensive than imaging suggests.

Oncoplastic Techniques
Two broad strategies for managing the defect left after excision, chosen based on tumour location and breast size/shape.
StrategyApproachTypical scenario
Volume displacementLocal tissue rearrangement to fill the defect, often combined with breast reshaping/reduction techniquesModerate-to-large breasts with reasonable glandular laxity
Volume replacementBringing in tissue from elsewhere (local perforator flaps) to fill the defectSmaller breasts, or a defect too large for local displacement alone

Radiotherapy — a mandatory partner, not an optional add-on

Adjuvant whole-breast radiotherapy is standard of care after breast-conserving surgery for invasive cancer, substantially reducing local recurrence rates — this is why radiotherapy eligibility is a genuine gating factor in deciding whether breast-conserving surgery is appropriate for a given patient.

Surgical Relevance
The decision points that determine whether a patient is offered BCS or steered toward mastectomy.

When mastectomy is preferred over BCS

  • Multicentric disease (multiple tumours in separate quadrants).
  • Unfavourable tumour-to-breast size ratio, where excision would leave an unacceptable cosmetic result.
  • Contraindication to radiotherapy.
  • Patient preference, after informed discussion of both options and their respective trade-offs.

Neoadjuvant therapy to enable BCS

In a patient who would otherwise need mastectomy due to tumour size, neoadjuvant chemotherapy (particularly effective in triple-negative and HER2-positive subtypes) can shrink the tumour enough to allow breast-conserving surgery — a key link back to the molecular subtype discussion in the Breast Cancer guide.

Viva pearl

If asked to justify offering BCS over mastectomy for an appropriate candidate, cite the landmark trials (NSABP B-06, Milan trials) demonstrating equivalent overall survival between BCS-plus-radiotherapy and mastectomy for early-stage disease — this equivalence is the entire justification for breast conservation as a legitimate oncological strategy, not just a cosmetic compromise.
