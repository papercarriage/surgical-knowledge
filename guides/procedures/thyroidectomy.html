Thyroidectomy

A step-by-step guide to the operation: indications, what to set up, the sequence of dissection that protects the RLN, EBSLN and parathyroids, and the post-operative problems you are expected to manage.

Types and Indications

OperationIndications
Hemithyroidectomy (lobectomy ± isthmusectomy)Solitary nodule, Thy3/4 diagnostic, low-risk cancer, toxic adenoma
Total thyroidectomyCancer needing total removal, Graves', MNG with bilateral disease, compressive goitre
Completion thyroidectomyAfter lobectomy when histology demands it
Near-total / subtotalRarely used now; leaves a small remnant, higher recurrence
Consent (Approximate Risks)

• Temporary RLN palsy about 5–10%; permanent about 1% or less

• Transient hypocalcaemia common after total (about 20–30%); permanent hypoparathyroidism about 1–3%

• Bleeding/haematoma about 1%, voice change (EBSLN), scar, infection, possible need for tracheostomy; lifelong levothyroxine after total; completion surgery if histology demands

Pre-operative Checklist

• Euthyroid; calcium, vitamin D; vocal cord assessment (voice change, previous neck surgery, cancer, retrosternal goitre)

• Imaging for retrosternal extension or cancer; group and save; MDT outcome documented

• IONM available; tube positioned with electrodes at the cords

⚠️ Exam Trap

The RLN is at highest risk at the ligament of Berry and at the tubercle of Zuckerkandl; the EBSLN at the superior pole. Mass ligation of the superior pedicle is how pitch is lost. Do not clip or ligate any structure in the tracheo-oesophageal groove until the nerve is identified.

Key Facts & Numbers

Set-up and operative sequence.

Set-up

• GA with nerve-monitoring tube; supine, shoulder roll, neck extended, head ring; slight head-up tilt (reverse Trendelenburg ~15–30°) to reduce venous pressure

• Eyes protected; arms by sides; skin prep chin to nipples; prophylactic antibiotics not routine

• Equipment: Kocher retractors, bipolar/harmonic/LigaSure, clips, nerve stimulator probe, magnification/loupes, drain optional

Operative Sequence

StepKey points
1. IncisionCollar (Kocher) incision, 2 finger-breadths above sternal notch in a skin crease
2. FlapsSubplatysmal flaps up to the thyroid cartilage and down to the sternal notch
3. Open the midlineDivide linea alba colli; retract (or divide high) the strap muscles
4. Mobilise the lobeDivide middle thyroid vein early to rotate the lobe medially
5. Upper poleLigate branches of the superior thyroid vessels individually on the gland (protect EBSLN); preserve superior parathyroid
6. RLNIdentify the nerve in the tracheo-oesophageal groove (triangle of Beahrs: inferior thyroid artery, carotid, thyroid), trace it to the larynx
7. Lower pole / inferior thyroid arteryLigate branches at the capsule; preserve parathyroids with their pedicle
8. Ligament of BerryCareful dissection with the nerve in view; avoid thermal spread
9. Remove and checkValsalva for haemostasis; inspect parathyroids; autotransplant any devascularised gland
10. CloseStrap muscles, platysma, skin; drain optional
⚠️ Exam Trap

Division of the inferior thyroid artery trunk devascularises the parathyroids; ligate its terminal branches at the capsule. If IONM signal is lost on the first side, stop and stage the contralateral side.

Surgical Relevance

Problems in recovery and how to manage them.

Neck Haematoma (Emergency)

• Tense swelling, stridor, dysphagia, pain; usually within the first 6 hours but can be later

• Open the wound at the bedside (remove skin sutures/clips, then strap layer) to decompress; oxygen; call anaesthetist; return to theatre for evacuation; do not wait for imaging

• Intubation first may be impossible because of laryngeal oedema

Hypocalcaemia

• Check calcium or PTH day 0/1; supplement on thresholds per unit protocol

• Symptomatic: IV calcium gluconate 10 mL of 10% over 10 min with ECG; then oral calcium and alfacalcidol

Voice and Airway

• Unilateral RLN palsy: hoarse, breathy; speech therapy, consider medialisation if persistent

• Bilateral palsy: stridor, airway emergency, reintubation/tracheostomy

Discharge and Follow-up

• Levothyroxine about 1.6 µg/kg/day after total thyroidectomy; TSH at 6–8 weeks

• Histology review at MDT; cancer follow-up as per risk

Clinical Pearl

Document the nerve identified, the IONM signal at the end of the operation, and parathyroid glands preserved or autotransplanted. This helps medicolegally and with follow-up.

Position for thyroidectomy? flip ↓
Supine with shoulder roll, neck extended, head ring, slight head-up tilt. Eyes protected.
Incision and level? flip ↓
Kocher collar incision in a skin crease, two finger-breadths above the sternal notch. Subplatysmal flaps.
First vessel usually divided to rotate the lobe medially? flip ↓
The middle thyroid vein (short and fragile, drains to IJV).
How to protect the EBSLN at the upper pole? flip ↓
Ligate superior thyroid vessel branches individually, close to the gland; never mass-ligate the pedicle.
How to ligate the inferior thyroid artery? flip ↓
At its branches on the thyroid capsule, not the main trunk, to preserve parathyroid blood supply.
Where is the RLN identified? flip ↓
Tracheo-oesophageal groove, in the triangle of Beahrs (inferior thyroid artery, carotid sheath, thyroid). Trace to the larynx at the ligament of Berry.
Consent risks for total thyroidectomy? flip ↓
RLN palsy (temporary 5–10%, permanent about 1%), hypocalcaemia, haematoma, voice change, scar, lifelong levothyroxine.
What do you do if IONM signal is lost on one side? flip ↓
Stop, check the equipment and nerve; stage the contralateral operation.
First step for neck haematoma with stridor? flip ↓
Open the wound at the bedside to decompress (skin, then strap layer), oxygen, call anaesthetist, theatre for evacuation.
What do you do with a devascularised parathyroid? flip ↓
Confirm it by frozen section, mince, and autotransplant into SCM (forearm if re-exploration likely).
Starting levothyroxine dose after total thyroidectomy? flip ↓
About 1.6 µg/kg/day; check TSH at 6–8 weeks.
When is completion thyroidectomy done? flip ↓
When histology after lobectomy shows cancer needing total removal (size, invasion, nodes).

A. A. Recurrent laryngeal nerve
B. B. Superior parathyroid gland

D. D. Open the wound at the bedside, then theatre
Airway compromise from haematoma requires immediate decompression: remove skin sutures/clips and release the strap layer, then evacuate in theatre.
Question 4 of 5Which is the safest way to deal with the inferior thyroid artery?
A. A. Ligate the main trunk laterally
B. B. Ligate its branches at the thyroid capsule
C. C. Leave it undivided
D. D. Diathermy the artery at the carotid sheath
Parathyroid glands are supplied by end-arteries from the inferior thyroid artery. Capsular ligation preserves them and keeps the plane safe relative to the RLN.
Question 5 of 5A patient is discharged day 1 after total thyroidectomy. Which statement is correct?
A. A. Start levothyroxine about 1.6 µg/kg/day and check TSH at 6–8 weeks
B. B. No thyroid hormone is needed
C. C. Start carbimazole
D. D. Check TSH the next day only
After total thyroidectomy the patient needs lifelong levothyroxine; the dose is about 1.6 µg/kg/day, titrated by TSH after 6–8 weeks (cancer patients may have a suppression target).
