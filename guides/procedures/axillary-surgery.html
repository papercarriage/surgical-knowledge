Sentinel Lymph Node Biopsy
Staging the axilla with the least morbidity possible — SLNB has transformed axillary management from routine clearance to targeted, selective surgery.

Technique

Dual mapping is standard practice: a radioisotope (injected pre-operatively, localised with a gamma probe intra-operatively) combined with a blue dye (injected at induction, visually identifying stained nodes and afferent lymphatics) — using both techniques together improves identification rate over either alone. The first-echelon node(s) draining the tumour are excised and sent for histopathological assessment, including intra-operative frozen section or touch-imprint cytology in some centres.

The Z0011 Principle & ALND Morbidity
A landmark shift in practice — knowing when ALND can safely be omitted, and the cost of performing it when it isn't needed.

ACOSOG Z0011 — omitting ALND

The practice-changing finding

In patients undergoing breast-conserving surgery with whole-breast radiotherapy, who have 1–2 positive sentinel nodes on SLNB, and a clinically node-negative axilla pre-operatively, completion ALND can be safely omitted without compromising oncological outcomes — radiotherapy adequately treats any residual nodal disease. This does not apply to patients undergoing mastectomy or with ≥3 positive nodes, and specific eligibility criteria must be met.

ALND morbidity

ComplicationNote
LymphoedemaThe most feared long-term complication — significantly more common after ALND than SLNB alone
SeromaCommon, usually self-limiting or managed with aspiration
Nerve injuryLong thoracic, thoracodorsal, intercostobrachial nerves at risk — see Axilla Anatomy guide
Reduced shoulder range of motionManaged with early physiotherapy
⚠️ Exam Trap

Z0011 criteria are narrow and specific — breast-conserving surgery, planned whole-breast radiotherapy, clinically node-negative axilla pre-operatively, and only 1–2 positive sentinel nodes. Applying the principle outside these criteria (e.g. mastectomy, ≥3 positive nodes, or no planned radiotherapy) is a common exam error.

Surgical Relevance
This guide connects directly to the axilla anatomy you've already covered — the nerves at risk are the same, the levels dissected are the same.

Applying the anatomy

Everything covered in the Axilla Anatomy & Lymph Node Levels guide is directly operative here — the long thoracic and thoracodorsal nerves must be identified and preserved during ALND, and the axillary vein remains the key superior landmark for the extent of dissection.

Why minimising unnecessary ALND matters

Because lymphoedema is a lifelong, often progressive complication with no reliable cure, the entire trajectory of axillary surgery research over the past two decades has been toward de-escalation — SLNB replacing routine ALND, and Z0011 further narrowing which node-positive patients still need it. Understanding this trajectory (rather than just the current rules) helps make sense of why axillary management keeps evolving.

Viva pearl

If asked to justify omitting ALND under Z0011 criteria, the expected answer isn't just "the trial said so" — it's that whole-breast radiotherapy (given as standard after BCS) adequately treats residual low-volume nodal disease, making completion ALND redundant in this specific, well-defined population.
