The Two Broad Strategies
Reconstruction options split fundamentally into using synthetic material versus the patient's own tissue — each with a distinct risk/benefit profile.

Implant-based reconstruction

  • Direct-to-implant: a permanent implant placed in a single stage at the time of mastectomy.
  • Tissue expander → implant: a temporary expander is placed first, gradually inflated over subsequent visits to stretch the skin envelope, then exchanged for a permanent implant in a second procedure.

Choosing Between Options
The decision integrates patient anatomy, oncological treatment plan, and patient preference.

⚠️ Exam Trap

Radiotherapy and implants are a genuinely poor combination — irradiated implant reconstructions have significantly higher rates of capsular contracture, infection, and revision surgery. This is a major reason delayed autologous reconstruction (or delayed implant reconstruction, after radiotherapy is complete) is often favoured when post-mastectomy radiotherapy is anticipated.

Surgical Relevance
Integrating reconstruction planning with the wider cancer treatment pathway.

Sequencing with oncological treatment

  • If post-mastectomy radiotherapy is anticipated (e.g. large tumours, extensive nodal disease), reconstruction is often deliberately delayed until radiotherapy is complete, or an autologous approach (more radiotherapy-tolerant) is favoured even if performed immediately.
  • If radiotherapy is not anticipated, immediate implant-based or autologous reconstruction at the time of mastectomy is a reasonable option, with better cosmetic and psychological outcomes than delayed reconstruction.

Patient factors

Body habitus (adequate donor tissue for autologous options), smoking status (significantly increases flap and wound complication risk), comorbidities (affecting fitness for longer autologous procedures), and patient preference all factor into the final decision — this is genuinely a shared decision-making process, not a fixed algorithm.

Viva pearl

If asked to counsel a patient anticipating post-mastectomy radiotherapy about reconstruction timing, the expected answer weighs the trade-off directly: immediate reconstruction gives a better initial cosmetic/psychological outcome but risks radiotherapy-related complications, particularly with implants — autologous tissue tolerates radiotherapy better, and delaying reconstruction until after radiotherapy avoids the interaction entirely at the cost of a longer overall reconstruction journey.
