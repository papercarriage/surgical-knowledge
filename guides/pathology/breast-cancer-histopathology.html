Grading the Tumour
Grade tells you how the cancer behaves under the microscope; receptor status tells you what will treat it. You need both.

Nottingham (Bloom-Richardson) grading system

Three features are each scored 1–3, then summed (total 3–9) to give an overall grade:

FeatureWhat's assessed
Tubule/gland formationHow much the tumour retains normal glandular architecture
Nuclear pleomorphismVariation in nuclear size and shape
Mitotic countNumber of mitoses per high-power field
Total scoreGrade
3–5Grade 1 (well differentiated)
6–7Grade 2 (moderately differentiated)
8–9Grade 3 (poorly differentiated)

Higher grade correlates with more aggressive behaviour and, generally, worse prognosis independent of stage.

Receptor Status
ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67 — the panel that drives systemic treatment selection.

Oestrogen and progesterone receptors (ER/PR)

Assessed by immunohistochemistry, typically reported as the percentage of tumour cells staining positive (or via a semi-quantitative system such as the Allred score). ER-positive tumours are candidates for endocrine therapy (tamoxifen, aromatase inhibitors).

HER2

IHC scoreInterpretation
0Negative
1+Negative (but now relevant as "HER2-low" — see below)
2+Equivocal — requires reflex testing with FISH/ISH for gene amplification
3+Positive

HER2-positive tumours are candidates for HER2-targeted therapy (e.g. trastuzumab).

⚠️ Exam Trap

An IHC score of 2+ is not a final result — it is equivocal and mandates reflex FISH/ISH testing for gene amplification before a HER2 status can be assigned. Don't treat 2+ as either positive or negative in isolation.

HER2-low — a newer, clinically relevant category

Tumours previously classified simply as "HER2-negative" (IHC 1+, or IHC 2+ with a negative ISH) are now recognised as a distinct HER2-low category, since the development of HER2-targeted antibody-drug conjugates effective even at this lower expression level — a genuinely newer development worth knowing given how quickly it has changed classification and treatment eligibility discussions.

Ki-67

A proliferation marker — the percentage of tumour cells staining positive reflects how rapidly the tumour is dividing, feeding into molecular subtype classification (helping distinguish Luminal A from Luminal B) and prognostic/treatment discussions.

Surgical Relevance
How this panel changes what happens on the ward and in theatre.

Why receptor status matters to the surgical trainee

  • Receptor status is determined from core biopsy tissue — this is one of the reasons core biopsy (which preserves architecture and yields enough tissue for IHC) is preferred over FNA (cytology only) for a new suspicious lesion.
  • Triple-negative or HER2-positive status is a common trigger for neoadjuvant chemotherapy, changing the surgical sequencing (surgery after systemic treatment rather than before).
  • Grade and receptor status, alongside stage, feed into MDT discussions about extent of surgery, radiotherapy fields, and whether genomic assays (e.g. Oncotype DX) are used to refine the chemotherapy decision in ER+/HER2− disease.

Discordance

If receptor status on core biopsy seems inconsistent with the clinical/radiological picture, or if neoadjuvant treatment is given, receptor status may be re-checked on the surgical specimen — a genuine possibility given intratumoural heterogeneity and potential change in expression after systemic therapy.

Viva pearl

If asked why core biopsy is preferred over FNA for a new breast lesion, the expected answer ties directly back to this guide: core biopsy preserves tissue architecture (enabling grading) and yields sufficient tissue for the full ER/PR/HER2/Ki-67 panel, whereas FNA provides cytology only.
