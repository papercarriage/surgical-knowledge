Axillary Boundaries & Contents
The anatomy underlying every axillary node dissection and sentinel node biopsy you'll assist in or perform this rotation.

Boundaries

WallStructure
ApexClavicle, 1st rib, superior border of scapula
BaseSkin and axillary fascia
AnteriorPectoralis major and minor
PosteriorSubscapularis, latissimus dorsi, teres major
MedialSerratus anterior, overlying the upper ribs
LateralIntertubercular (bicipital) groove of the humerus

Contents

Axillary vessels (artery and vein), cords and branches of the brachial plexus, 20–30 lymph nodes, the axillary tail of the breast, and a variable amount of fat.

Landmark to know

The axillary vein is the key intra-operative landmark for the superior extent of a level I/II dissection — nodal tissue is cleared inferior to it, minimising injury to the vein and structures above it.

Lymph Node Levels & Nerves at Risk
Levels are defined relative to pectoralis minor — a simple, exam-favourite classification.

Berg levels

LevelLocation relative to pec minorNote
Level ILateral to pectoralis minorMost commonly involved level; cleared in a standard axillary sample
Level IIDeep to (posterior to) pectoralis minorIncludes the interpectoral (Rotter's) nodes between pec major and minor
Level IIIMedial to pectoralis minor (apical)Cleared only for extensive nodal disease — highest recurrence risk if involved
⚠️ Exam Trap

Rotter's (interpectoral) nodes sit between pectoralis major and minor and are classified as Level II, not a separate category — a commonly missed detail.

Surgical Relevance
This is the anatomy you rehearse mentally before every axillary case.

Intra-operative nerve identification

  • The long thoracic nerve runs along the medial wall of the axilla on the surface of serratus anterior — identify and preserve it early during medial dissection.
  • The thoracodorsal nerve runs with the thoracodorsal vessels along the posterior wall toward latissimus dorsi — trace the vessels to find the nerve.
  • The intercostobrachial nerve crosses the operative field superficially; some surgeons preserve it where oncologically safe, others sacrifice it routinely — practice varies, and this remains a genuine area of surgeon preference rather than a fixed rule.

Extent of dissection

Sentinel lymph node biopsy samples the first-echelon node(s), usually within Level I. Axillary lymph node dissection (ALND) for confirmed nodal disease typically clears Levels I and II; Level III (apical) dissection is reserved for extensive disease, given both the added morbidity and the diminishing yield of clinically relevant nodes at that level.

Viva pearl

If asked to describe how you'd protect the long thoracic and thoracodorsal nerves intra-operatively, the expected answer references their consistent anatomical relationships — long thoracic nerve on serratus anterior along the medial wall, thoracodorsal nerve running with the thoracodorsal vessels toward latissimus dorsi — not just "I'd be careful."
