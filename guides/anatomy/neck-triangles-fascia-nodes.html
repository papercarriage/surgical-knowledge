Neck Triangles, Fascia & Nodal Levels

Three frameworks you must hold at once: the triangles (surface and operative landmarks), the fascial planes (where infection and dissection go), and the nodal levels (where cancer goes and what a neck dissection removes). SCM is the dividing line for all of them.

Triangles of the Neck

Anterior Triangle

• Boundaries: midline, anterior border of SCM, lower border of mandible

• Submental: between the anterior bellies of digastric and the hyoid. Level IA nodes

• Submandibular (digastric): mandible and both bellies of digastric. Submandibular gland, facial artery, hypoglossal and lingual nerves, Level IB nodes

• Carotid: posterior belly of digastric, superior belly of omohyoid, anterior border of SCM. Carotid bifurcation, IJV, vagus, hypoglossal nerve, ansa cervicalis, Level II–III nodes

• Muscular: midline, superior belly of omohyoid, SCM. Strap muscles, thyroid, larynx, trachea

Posterior Triangle

• Boundaries: posterior border of SCM, anterior border of trapezius, middle third of clavicle. Apex at the occipital bone

• Roof: investing layer of deep cervical fascia. Floor: prevertebral fascia over splenius capitis, levator scapulae, middle and posterior scalenes

• Inferior belly of omohyoid splits it into a large occipital and a small supraclavicular (subclavian) triangle

• Contents: spinal accessory nerve (CN XI), cervical plexus branches, roots and trunks of brachial plexus, external jugular vein, 3rd part of subclavian artery, transverse cervical and suprascapular vessels, Level V nodes

Clinical Pearl

Erb's point (mid-point of the posterior border of SCM) is where the four cutaneous branches of the cervical plexus emerge: lesser occipital, great auricular, transverse cervical, supraclavicular. CN XI also passes near this point, so it is the landmark to find the nerve.

Cervical Fascia

Deep Cervical Fascia: Three Layers + Carotid Sheath

• Investing layer: encircles the neck, splitting to enclose SCM, trapezius, and the submandibular and parotid glands

• Pretracheal layer: muscular part encloses the strap muscles; visceral part encloses the thyroid, trachea and oesophagus. Continuous inferiorly with the fibrous pericardium

• Prevertebral layer: encloses vertebral column and its muscles. Forms the floor of the posterior triangle. Phrenic nerve and brachial plexus lie deep to it

• Carotid sheath: contributions from all three layers. Contains common/internal carotid artery, IJV and vagus. The sympathetic chain lies behind the sheath, not in it. Ansa cervicalis is embedded in the anterior wall

Deep Neck Spaces

• Retropharyngeal space: between the buccopharyngeal fascia and the alar fascia. Closed inferiorly at about T1–T2

• Danger space: between alar and prevertebral fascia. Open through to the posterior mediastinum (to the diaphragm)

• Submandibular space: sublingual and submandibular compartments above and below mylohyoid. Bilateral involvement with the submental space is Ludwig's angina

⚠️ Exam Trap

The danger space, not the retropharyngeal space, provides the direct route to the posterior mediastinum. Infections from lower 2nd/3rd molars spread to the submandibular space because their roots lie below the mylohyoid line.

Landmark Numbers & Names

Must-Know

• Cutaneous plexus branches: lesser occipital C2, great auricular C2–3, transverse cervical C2–3, supraclavicular C3–4

• Phrenic nerve: C3–5, on anterior scalene under prevertebral fascia

• Virchow's node: left supraclavicular (Level IV/VB). Troisier's sign with abdominal malignancy

• Delphian node: prelaryngeal, Level VI

• Jugulodigastric node: Level II, tonsillar drainage

• Thoracic duct enters the left venous angle (IJV/subclavian junction), at Level IV/VB

⚠️ Exam Trap

Level VI is central and bounded by the carotids; it is not part of a standard lateral (I–V) neck dissection. Level IIB is posterior to CN XI, and Level V is divided at the cricoid, not the hyoid.

Surgical Relevance

Where this anatomy bites you in theatre, clinic and ED.

Incisions and Planes

Access

• Collar (Kocher) incision in a skin crease, about two finger-breadths above the sternal notch, for thyroid and central neck work

• Raise subplatysmal flaps; the linea alba colli (midline raphe between straps) is the avascular entry

• Take care with the anterior jugular veins in the suprasternal space of Burns

• Neck dissection: apron or modified Schobinger incisions, flaps raised deep to platysma

⚠️ Exam Trap

An excisional biopsy of a posterior triangle node is the classic way to injure CN XI. Examine for a drooping shoulder, document shoulder function before surgery, and counsel on the risk in consent.

Complications of Neck Dissection

Know These

• Chyle leak, mainly after left level IV/VB dissection (thoracic duct). Milky drain fluid with high triglycerides. Low-fat or MCT diet, then re-exploration if high output

• Shoulder dysfunction, even with XI preserved

• Haematoma and airway compromise

• Marginal mandibular and Horner's as above

Deep Neck Infections

Airway First

• Ludwig's angina: bilateral submandibular, sublingual and submental involvement. Tongue pushed up and back, trismus, drooling. Most commonly from lower molars. Secure the airway early (senior anaesthetist, awake fibreoptic or surgical airway ready), IV antibiotics, drainage

• Retropharyngeal abscess: children, dysphagia, neck stiffness, stridor. Spread via the danger space to the posterior mediastinum causes mediastinitis

Boundaries of the posterior triangle? flip ↓
Posterior border of SCM, anterior border of trapezius, middle third of clavicle. Apex at the occipital bone. Roof is investing fascia, floor is prevertebral fascia.
What divides the posterior triangle and into what? flip ↓
The inferior belly of omohyoid divides it into the occipital triangle (large) and the supraclavicular triangle (small).
Four subdivisions of the anterior triangle? flip ↓
Submental, submandibular (digastric), carotid, muscular.
Which nerves emerge at Erb's point? flip ↓
Lesser occipital, great auricular, transverse cervical and supraclavicular nerves (cervical plexus cutaneous branches), at the mid-point of the posterior border of SCM. CN XI is also close to this point.
What does the carotid sheath contain, and what is not in it? flip ↓
Common/internal carotid artery, IJV, vagus nerve. The sympathetic trunk lies behind it, not within. Ansa cervicalis is embedded in its anterior wall.
What does the pretracheal fascia enclose? flip ↓
Muscular part: strap muscles. Visceral part: thyroid, trachea, oesophagus. Continuous inferiorly with fibrous pericardium.
What is the danger space and why does it matter? flip ↓
The space between alar and prevertebral fascia. Open down to the posterior mediastinum (diaphragm), so infection tracks to mediastinitis.
What is Ludwig's angina? flip ↓
Bilateral cellulitis of submandibular, sublingual and submental spaces, usually from lower molars (roots below the mylohyoid line). Tongue elevated, trismus, drooling. Airway first.
Boundaries of Level VI and what is in it? flip ↓
Hyoid above, suprasternal notch below, common carotids laterally. Pretracheal, paratracheal and prelaryngeal (Delphian) nodes. First echelon for thyroid cancer.
How are Level IIA and IIB distinguished? flip ↓
By CN XI. IIA is anterior to (or in contact with) the nerve; IIB is posterior to it. Both run from skull base to hyoid.
What does a radical neck dissection remove versus a modified radical? flip ↓
Radical: Levels I–V plus SCM, IJV and CN XI. Modified radical: Levels I–V while preserving one or more of those three (type III preserves all).
Why is chyle leak mainly a left-sided complication? flip ↓
The thoracic duct enters the left venous angle (IJV/subclavian junction) at Level IV/VB. Milky drain fluid with high triglycerides. Start dietary measures, re-explore if high output.

Question 1 of 5A patient has an excisional biopsy of a lymph node in the posterior triangle. Afterwards the shoulder droops and she cannot abduct the arm above the horizontal. Which nerve was injured?
A. Long thoracic nerve
B. Spinal accessory nerve
C. Suprascapular nerve
D. Phrenic nerve
CN XI runs superficially across the posterior triangle to supply trapezius, so it is vulnerable to node biopsy. Loss of trapezius causes shoulder droop and weak abduction above horizontal. Long thoracic injury gives a winged scapula without these features; phrenic injury affects the diaphragm.
Question 2 of 5Which description fits Level VI of the neck?
A. Mandible to hyoid, anterior to the submandibular gland
B. Skull base to hyoid, around the jugular vein
C. Hyoid to suprasternal notch, between the common carotid arteries
D. Posterior triangle below the cricoid
Level VI is the central compartment, from the hyoid to the suprasternal notch, bounded laterally by the common carotids. It is the first echelon for thyroid cancer. Option A is Level I, B is Level II and D is Level VB.
Question 3 of 5A 62-year-old has weight loss and a hard left supraclavicular node. Which statement is correct?
A. It is a Level II node and suggests nasopharyngeal carcinoma
B. It is a Delphian node draining the thyroid
C. It is a Level VI node draining the larynx
D. It is Virchow's node, in Level IV/VB, and suggests an abdominal or thoracic primary
Virchow's node (Troisier's sign) is the left supraclavicular node near the termination of the thoracic duct. It classically signals gastric or other abdominal/thoracic malignancy. Delphian is prelaryngeal; Level VI is central; Level II is high in the jugular chain.
Question 4 of 5A child has a retropharyngeal abscess that is not drained. Through which space can infection reach the posterior mediastinum most directly?
A. Carotid sheath
B. Danger space
C. Pretracheal space
D. Masticator space
The danger space lies between the alar and prevertebral fascia and runs to the diaphragm, providing a direct route to the posterior mediastinum. The retropharyngeal space itself ends around T1–T2. The pretracheal space can reach the anterior mediastinum.
Question 5 of 5A 40-year-old with a lower molar infection has bilateral submandibular swelling, an elevated tongue, drooling and trismus. What is the priority?
A. Early senior airway assessment and securing the airway
B. Oral antibiotics and dental review in a week
C. CT neck while lying flat, then decide
D. Needle aspiration in the emergency department
This is Ludwig's angina. The tongue is displaced up and back and oedema can obstruct the airway rapidly. Call senior anaesthetic and ENT help, and plan an awake fibreoptic or surgical airway. IV antibiotics and surgical drainage follow. Lying flat for CT is unsafe until the airway is controlled.
