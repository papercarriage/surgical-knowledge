Definition & Causes
Proliferation of glandular breast tissue in males — usually benign, but the workup exists to catch the exceptions.

True gynaecomastia vs pseudogynaecomastia

  • True gynaecomastia: proliferation of actual glandular tissue, palpable as a firm, rubbery, mobile, centrally-located disc beneath the areola.
  • Pseudogynaecomastia: fat deposition without true glandular proliferation, associated with obesity — soft throughout, no discrete central disc.

Workup & Distinguishing Male Breast Cancer
Gynaecomastia is common and usually benign — but every case deserves a moment's consideration of male breast cancer before reassuring.

Basic workup

  • Detailed drug history — often the culprit and easily reversible.
  • Examine for signs of liver disease, thyroid disease, and testicular masses (examine the testes in every case).
  • Relevant bloods where indicated: LFTs, TFTs, and hormonal panel (testosterone, oestradiol, LH/FSH, hCG) if a pathological cause is suspected or the presentation is atypical.

⚠️ Exam Trap

Male breast cancer is rare but real — any eccentric, hard, fixed male breast lump, especially unilateral, needs triple assessment rather than being assumed to be gynaecomastia.

Surgical Relevance
Most gynaecomastia doesn't need an operation — know when it does.

Indications for surgery

  • Persistent gynaecomastia beyond the expected resolution window (e.g. pubertal gynaecomastia persisting beyond ~2 years).
  • Significant symptoms (pain, tenderness) or psychosocial distress.
  • Patient preference for cosmetic correction once reversible causes have been addressed/excluded.

Surgical options

  • Subcutaneous mastectomy: removes the glandular disc, typically via a peri-areolar incision.
  • Liposuction: used alone for predominantly fatty (pseudogynaecomastia) presentations, or combined with subcutaneous mastectomy for mixed glandular/fatty cases.
Viva pearl

If asked to work up a new gynaecomastia case, the expected structure is: drug history first (commonest reversible cause), then examine the testes (don't skip this), then consider liver/thyroid/renal causes, keeping male breast cancer in mind throughout rather than as an afterthought.
