Classification & Risk
The spectrum from risk marker to invasive malignancy, and the framework used to stage and subtype it.

Risk factors

  • Genetic: BRCA1/BRCA2 mutations (also raise ovarian cancer risk), strong family history, other syndromes (Li-Fraumeni, Cowden).
  • Hormonal/reproductive: early menarche, late menopause, nulliparity or late first pregnancy, hormone replacement therapy, combined oral contraceptive use (small increase).
  • Other: increasing age (the single biggest risk factor), obesity (post-menopausal), alcohol, prior chest radiotherapy, personal history of breast cancer or high-risk lesions (LCIS, atypical hyperplasia).

The spectrum of disease

EntityNature
LCIS (lobular carcinoma in situ)A risk marker for future cancer in either breast, not a direct precursor lesion — an important distinction from DCIS
DCIS (ductal carcinoma in situ)A true non-invasive precursor confined by the basement membrane — if untreated, a proportion progress to invasive cancer
Invasive ductal carcinoma (no special type, NST)Most common invasive type, ~70–80% of cases
Invasive lobular carcinoma~10% of cases — often more diffuse/infiltrative growth, harder to detect on imaging and exam, higher rate of bilaterality
Special types (tubular, mucinous, medullary)Generally better prognosis than NST
⚠️ Exam Trap

LCIS is not treated like DCIS. Classic LCIS is a risk marker managed with surveillance (or risk-reduction discussion), not routine wide excision — whereas DCIS is a genuine precursor lesion requiring excision, often with radiotherapy. The exception is pleomorphic LCIS, a more aggressive variant managed more like DCIS.

Screening

Population mammographic screening programmes aim to detect cancer before it's clinically palpable. Findings are reported using the BI-RADS classification (0–6), which standardises risk communication and next-step recommendations between radiologists and clinicians.

Staging & Molecular Subtypes
Staging tells you extent; subtyping tells you biology — you need both to plan treatment.

TNM staging — the framework

ComponentKey thresholds
T (tumour)Broadly staged by size: Tis (in situ), T1 (≤2cm), T2 (2–5cm), T3 (>5cm), T4 (chest wall/skin involvement, inflammatory carcinoma)
N (nodes)N0 (none), N1 (mobile ipsilateral axillary), N2 (fixed/matted axillary, or internal mammary without axillary), N3 (infraclavicular/supraclavicular/internal mammary + axillary)
M (metastasis)M0 vs M1 (distant metastasis present)

Molecular subtypes

Defined by ER, PR, HER2, and Ki-67 (proliferation index) — this classification drives systemic treatment choice as much as, or more than, TNM stage:

SubtypeReceptor profileBehaviour
Luminal AER+/PR+, HER2−, low Ki-67Best prognosis, slow-growing, strongly endocrine-responsive
Luminal BER+, HER2±, higher Ki-67More proliferative than Luminal A, still endocrine-responsive
HER2-enrichedER−/PR−, HER2+Aggressive but highly responsive to HER2-targeted therapy (e.g. trastuzumab)
Triple-negativeER−/PR−/HER2−No targeted endocrine/HER2 therapy available — chemotherapy-dependent, higher early recurrence risk
Surgical Relevance
How subtype and stage translate into the actual treatment pathway you'll see on the ward and in MDT.

Triple assessment before any treatment decision

Every suspected breast cancer goes through clinical examination, imaging, and tissue diagnosis (core biopsy) before a management plan is finalised — see the dedicated Triple Assessment guide for detail.

Neoadjuvant therapy

  • Considered for large tumours (to downstage and enable breast-conserving surgery), and preferentially in triple-negative or HER2+ subtypes, which tend to respond well to systemic therapy.
  • Response can be assessed clinically and radiologically during treatment — a poor responder may prompt a change in surgical plan.

MDT-driven planning

Surgical decisions (breast-conserving surgery vs mastectomy, axillary staging strategy) are made alongside oncology and radiology input at multidisciplinary team meetings, integrating stage, subtype, patient factors, and preference.

Viva pearl

If asked "why does receptor status matter more than tumour size for some decisions" — the expected answer is that molecular subtype predicts response to systemic therapy (endocrine therapy for ER+, trastuzumab for HER2+, chemotherapy-dependence for triple-negative) independent of anatomical stage, and increasingly drives neoadjuvant strategy.
