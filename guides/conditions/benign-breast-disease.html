The ANDI Framework
Most benign breast presentations are best understood as Aberrations of Normal Development and Involution (ANDI) — a useful mental model for why so many benign findings are age-linked.

The common presentations

Mastalgia

  • Cyclical: linked to the menstrual cycle, typically bilateral, hormonally driven — usually managed conservatively (supportive bra, analgesia, lifestyle).
  • Non-cyclical: not linked to the cycle, may be musculoskeletal (chest wall/costochondral) or breast-sourced — warrants a search for a focal cause.
⚠️ Exam Trap

A fibroadenoma that is large or rapidly growing, particularly in an older patient, must be biopsied to exclude a phyllodes tumour — the two can look identical clinically and even on imaging, but management differs fundamentally (see Key Facts).

Phyllodes Tumour & Nipple Discharge
Two areas where "benign-looking" doesn't mean "benign management."

Phyllodes tumour

  • A fibroepithelial tumour, similar in origin to a fibroadenoma but with a distinct stromal component — classified as benign, borderline, or malignant based on histological features.
  • Even "benign" phyllodes tumours have a tendency to recur locally if inadequately excised.
  • Management: wide local excision with a margin (typically ~1cm), not enucleation. Simple enucleation (as might be done for a fibroadenoma) leaves microscopic tumour behind and drives local recurrence.

Unilateral, spontaneous, blood-stained single-duct discharge is the classic pattern prompting investigation for an intraductal papilloma (most common cause of blood-stained discharge) or, less commonly, underlying malignancy.

Surgical Relevance
When to reassure, when to biopsy, and when to operate.

Indications for excision or biopsy

  • Fibroadenoma: excision considered if large (>3cm), growing, symptomatic, patient preference, or diagnostic uncertainty (to exclude phyllodes tumour) — many are managed conservatively with imaging follow-up if classically benign on triple assessment.
  • Simple cyst: aspirate if symptomatic; if the aspirate is blood-stained, or a residual mass persists after aspiration, further workup (imaging, biopsy) is needed to exclude an underlying lesion.
  • Pathological nipple discharge: triple assessment plus duct exploration/microdochectomy if a papilloma or other duct lesion is suspected.

Duct ectasia vs periductal mastitis

These sit on a spectrum — duct ectasia (dilated, shortened ducts, typically peri/post-menopausal, associated with thick discharge) can predispose to periductal mastitis (typically younger, smoking-associated, presenting with peri-areolar inflammation/abscess) — see the dedicated Mastitis & Breast Abscess guide for management of the acute presentation.

Viva pearl

If asked "why not just enucleate a phyllodes tumour like you would a fibroadenoma" — the expected answer is that phyllodes tumours, even histologically benign ones, have a real tendency toward local recurrence if margins aren't clear, unlike fibroadenomas.
