Home › Conditions › Paget's Disease of the Nipple
Presentation & Pathology
An eczematous-looking nipple change that is, until proven otherwise, a cancer diagnosis.

Clinical presentation

Eczematous, scaly, erythematous change of the nipple-areolar complex — itching, burning, or nipple discharge/bleeding may accompany it. Classically, the change begins on the nipple itself and spreads to involve the areola, whereas eczema typically involves the areola first (or affects both breasts symmetrically).

Underlying pathology

Not a primary skin condition

Paget's disease of the nipple is associated with an underlying DCIS or invasive carcinoma in the great majority of cases. The epidermis contains characteristic Paget cells — large, pale-staining malignant cells — which are believed to migrate from an underlying intraductal carcinoma along the duct system into the epidermis.

⚠️ Exam Trap

A nipple "eczema" that is unilateral and does not respond to topical steroids should never be assumed to be dermatological — this combination is the classic trigger for biopsy to exclude Paget's disease.

Diagnosis & Distinguishing Features
The key discriminator from true eczema, and how the diagnosis is confirmed.

Paget's disease vs nipple eczema

FeaturePaget's diseaseEczema
LateralityUnilateralOften bilateral
Starting pointNipple first, spreading to areolaAreola first, or affects skin more broadly

Diagnosis

Confirmed by punch or wedge biopsy of the nipple, demonstrating Paget cells within the epidermis on histology. Mammography and further breast imaging are performed to characterise the underlying lesion, even if no mass is palpable clinically.

Surgical Relevance
Management is dictated entirely by the underlying disease, not the nipple change itself.

Management follows the underlying cancer

  • If the underlying disease is localised DCIS confined near the nipple: central excision (removing the nipple-areolar complex and underlying duct tissue) with radiotherapy can be an option.
  • If there is more extensive DCIS or an invasive component: mastectomy is often required, following the same staging and treatment principles as any invasive/extensive DCIS presentation.
  • Axillary staging follows the same logic as for any invasive component identified.

The take-home clinical habit

Any unilateral, persistent nipple skin change — regardless of how convincingly "eczematous" it looks — should prompt at minimum a referral for triple assessment including nipple biopsy, rather than a trial of topical steroids in isolation.

Viva pearl

If asked "how do you distinguish Paget's disease from eczema," the expected answer leads with laterality and steroid response, not just "biopsy it" — showing you understand the clinical reasoning that triggers the biopsy in the first place.
