Hyperthyroidism

Thyrotoxicosis is managed medically first, but the surgical trainee needs the causes, the indications for thyroidectomy, how to prepare the patient safely, and how to recognise and treat thyroid storm.

Clinical Features

• Weight loss with good appetite, heat intolerance, palpitations (AF), tremor, sweating, anxiety, diarrhoea, oligomenorrhoea

• Signs: tachycardia, fine tremor, warm moist skin, lid lag and lid retraction; exophthalmos and ophthalmoplegia are Graves-specific

⚠️ Exam Trap

Orbitopathy, pretibial myxoedema and acropachy are features of Graves' only, not of toxic nodular disease. Radioiodine can worsen active orbitopathy (give steroid cover) and is contraindicated in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

Key Facts & Numbers

Investigations, drugs and thyroid storm.

Investigations

• TSH suppressed, raised fT4 and/or fT3; TSH receptor antibody (TRAb) positive in Graves'

• Technetium or iodine uptake scan if cause unclear; ultrasound for nodules

• FBC (agranulocytosis risk), LFTs, ECG, calcium

Treatment Options

OptionKey points
Beta blocker (propranolol)Rapid symptom control; also reduces T4 to T3 conversion
CarbimazoleTitration or block-and-replace for 12–18 months. Warn: sore throat or fever means urgent FBC (agranulocytosis). Teratogenic risk in first trimester
PropylthiouracilPreferred in first trimester and thyroid storm. Risk of severe hepatotoxicity
RadioiodineDefinitive; contraindicated in pregnancy/breastfeeding; avoid pregnancy for 6 months afterwards; may worsen eye disease
ThyroidectomyDefinitive and rapid; see indications below
Indications for Surgery

• Large or compressive goitre; suspected or proven malignancy; active or severe orbitopathy; failure or relapse after drug therapy; patient preference; pregnancy with drug intolerance (second trimester)

• Toxic MNG and toxic adenoma: surgery or radioiodine are definitive

⚠️ Exam Trap

Carbimazole: agranulocytosis presents as sore throat. PTU: hepatotoxicity. Before surgery, patients must be biochemically euthyroid; operating on an uncontrolled thyrotoxic patient risks thyroid storm.

Surgical Relevance

Preparing the thyrotoxic patient and managing storm.

Pre-operative Preparation

• Achieve euthyroidism with carbimazole ± beta blocker (confirm normal fT4 and fT3)

• Lugol's iodine (or potassium iodide) for 7–10 days pre-op reduces gland vascularity and blood loss (Wolff-Chaikoff effect). Give after antithyroid drug

• Continue beta blocker through the operation

• Pre-op laryngoscopy; calcium and vitamin D

Operation

• Total or near-total thyroidectomy is standard for Graves' (lower recurrence than subtotal)

• Highly vascular gland: control superior pole vessels individually, preserve parathyroids and RLN; consider drain and meticulous haemostasis

• Post-op: calcium monitoring (higher risk of hypocalcaemia in Graves'), stop antithyroid drugs, start levothyroxine

Thyroid Storm

• Fever, tachycardia, agitation or delirium, diarrhoea, heart failure; precipitated by surgery, infection, trauma, stopping drugs, iodine load

• Management: ABC and cooling; IV fluids; propranolol; PTU (blocks synthesis and conversion); Lugol's iodine given at least 1 hour after PTU; hydrocortisone; treat precipitant; ICU

• Avoid aspirin (displaces thyroxine from binding proteins); use paracetamol

⚠️ Exam Trap

Give iodine after the antithyroid drug: iodine given first can supply substrate for more hormone. In storm, use paracetamol, not aspirin.

Question 1 of 5A 32-year-old woman with thyrotoxicosis has exophthalmos and pretibial myxoedema. What is the most likely diagnosis?
A. A. Toxic multinodular goitre
B. B. Toxic adenoma

Question 2 of 5Two weeks after starting carbimazole, a patient develops a sore throat and fever. What is the immediate step?
A. A. Reassure and give paracetamol
B. B. Stop carbimazole and check an urgent FBC
C. C. Increase the dose
D. D. Switch to radioiodine now
Agranulocytosis is a rare, serious effect of thionamides. Stop the drug and check the neutrophil count urgently; manage neutropenic sepsis if present.
Question 3 of 5A thyrotoxic patient is prepared for thyroidectomy. Which is correct?
A. A. Give Lugol's iodine before starting carbimazole
B. B. Stop beta blockers 24 hours pre-op
C. C. Operate while TSH is suppressed to avoid recurrence
D. D. Render euthyroid, then give Lugol's iodine for 7–10 days to reduce vascularity
The patient should be euthyroid before surgery. Iodine is added later to reduce gland vascularity. Beta blockers continue to cover the peri-operative period.
Question 4 of 5In thyroid storm, which analgesic/antipyretic should be avoided?
A. A. Aspirin
B. B. Paracetamol
C. C. External cooling
D. D. IV fluids
Aspirin displaces thyroxine from its binding proteins, raising free hormone. Use paracetamol and cooling.
Question 5 of 5Which patient is a clear indication for surgery rather than radioiodine?
A. A. Elderly with small adenoma
B. B. Graves' with a large compressive goitre and active orbitopathy
C. C. Postpartum thyroiditis
D. D. Mild subclinical hyperthyroidism
Compressive goitre and severe/active eye disease favour surgery. Thyroiditis is self-limiting; subclinical disease is usually observed.
