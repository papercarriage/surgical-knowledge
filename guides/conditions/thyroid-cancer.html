Thyroid Cancer

Four histological types behave in four different ways. Know the identifying pathology, the route of spread, and what each means for the operation and follow-up.

The Four Types

Type%FeaturesSpread
Papillary~80%Orphan Annie eye nuclei, nuclear grooves, psammoma bodies; BRAF V600E; radiation-associated (RET/PTC). Young women; excellent prognosisLymphatic (central then lateral nodes)
Follicular~10–15%Capsular and vascular invasion define malignancy; RAS mutations. Hürthle cell variant. Iodine deficiencyHaematogenous (bone, lung)
Medullary~5%Parafollicular C cells; calcitonin, CEA; amyloid stroma. 25% familial: RET mutation (MEN2A, MEN2B, FMTC)Lymphatic and haematogenous
AnaplasticUnder 2%Elderly, rapidly enlarging mass, airway compromise; very poor prognosisLocal invasion, distant
Syndromes

• MEN2A: MTC + phaeochromocytoma + primary hyperparathyroidism

• MEN2B: MTC + phaeochromocytoma + mucosal neuromas + marfanoid habitus (no hyperparathyroidism)

• Familial adenomatous polyposis, Cowden, and Carney complex also raise follicular/papillary risk

⚠️ Exam Trap

Papillary spreads via lymphatics; follicular via the blood. Medullary cancer is of C cells and is not iodine-avid, so radioactive iodine is useless. Always exclude phaeochromocytoma (plasma metanephrines) before thyroid surgery in MEN2.

Key Facts & Numbers

Staging, treatment by type and follow-up.

Management Summary

TypeSurgeryAdjuncts / follow-up
Papillary, low risk (under 1 cm)Lobectomy (or active surveillance in selected cases)TSH in range; ultrasound follow-up
Papillary / follicular, 1–4 cmLobectomy or total thyroidectomy by riskRAI selected by risk
Over 4 cm, extrathyroidal extension, nodes, bilateral, radiation historyTotal thyroidectomy ± central neck dissectionRAI ablation, TSH suppression, thyroglobulin follow-up
MedullaryTotal thyroidectomy + central neck dissection (level VI)Calcitonin, CEA; screen RET, family, phaeochromocytoma
AnaplasticRarely resectable; airway (tracheostomy, stent)Chemoradiotherapy, palliative care
Neck Dissection

• Central (level VI): therapeutic for clinically involved nodes; prophylactic is selective (higher-risk papillary, MTC)

• Lateral (II–V): for FNAC-proven lateral nodal metastases; preserve CN XI, IJV and SCM if possible

Follow-up

• Thyroglobulin (with anti-thyroglobulin antibodies) after total thyroidectomy ± RAI; neck ultrasound

• TSH suppression for intermediate and high risk; levothyroxine lifelong after total thyroidectomy

• Medullary: calcitonin and CEA doubling times

⚠️ Exam Trap

Follicular carcinoma is diagnosed on histology, not FNAC. Anaplastic cancer is not treated with a thyroidectomy "to cure"; the priority is airway and palliation. A raised thyroglobulin after total thyroidectomy implies residual or recurrent disease (check TgAb).

Surgical Relevance

Operative principles when the diagnosis is cancer.

Planning

• MDT-driven. Staging ultrasound of the central and lateral neck; CT/MRI for suspected extrathyroidal extension or retrosternal disease

• Pre-op vocal cord assessment (laryngoscopy). Exclude phaeochromocytoma if MTC/MEN2

• Calcium and vitamin D; discuss parathyroid preservation and autotransplantation

Operation

• Total thyroidectomy: capsular dissection, identify and preserve RLN and parathyroids (autotransplant any devascularised gland), IONM

• Central neck dissection removes levels VI nodal tissue; highest risk is hypoparathyroidism and RLN injury

• If the nerve is invaded: shave off a functioning nerve from tumour where oncologically safe; sacrifice only a non-functioning nerve

• Completion thyroidectomy within days or after weeks if histology after lobectomy demands it

Complications

• RLN palsy, hypoparathyroidism, haematoma, chyle leak (left lateral), CN XI injury in lateral dissection

Clinical Pearl

MEN2B (RET M918T): prophylactic thyroidectomy in the first year of life; MEN2A (high-risk codons): by age 5 or earlier guided by calcitonin; operate on the phaeochromocytoma first, with alpha-blockade before beta-blockade.

Most common thyroid cancer and its histological signs? flip ↓
Papillary (~80%). Orphan Annie eye (ground-glass) nuclei, nuclear grooves, psammoma bodies; BRAF V600E.
Route of spread: papillary vs follicular? flip ↓
Papillary: lymphatic (cervical nodes). Follicular: haematogenous (bone, lung).
How is follicular carcinoma diagnosed? flip ↓
On histology: capsular and/or vascular invasion. FNAC cannot distinguish adenoma from carcinoma.
Which cell does medullary thyroid cancer arise from, and its markers? flip ↓
Parafollicular C cells; calcitonin and CEA; amyloid in stroma. Not iodine-avid.
MEN2A vs MEN2B? flip ↓
MEN2A: MTC, phaeochromocytoma, primary hyperparathyroidism. MEN2B: MTC, phaeochromocytoma, mucosal neuromas, marfanoid habitus.
Gene in familial MTC? flip ↓
RET proto-oncogene mutation (activating).
What to check before thyroidectomy for MTC? flip ↓
Plasma/urine metanephrines to exclude phaeochromocytoma; operate on phaeo first with alpha then beta blockade.
Features of anaplastic thyroid cancer? flip ↓
Elderly, rapidly enlarging hard mass, stridor/dysphagia, local invasion; median survival months. Airway and palliation.
Indications for total thyroidectomy in differentiated cancer? flip ↓
Over 4 cm, extrathyroidal extension, nodal or distant metastases, bilateral disease, prior radiation, aggressive histology.
Role of radioactive iodine? flip ↓
Ablates remnant and micrometastases in intermediate/high-risk papillary and follicular cancer. Needs TSH stimulation (withdrawal or rhTSH).
Tumour marker for follow-up of differentiated thyroid cancer? flip ↓
Thyroglobulin (with anti-Tg antibodies), after total thyroidectomy ± RAI.
Which neck level is central dissection? flip ↓
Level VI (hyoid to suprasternal notch, between carotids). Risks: hypoparathyroidism and RLN injury.

Question 1 of 5A histology report describes optically clear (Orphan Annie) nuclei, nuclear grooves and psammoma bodies. What is the diagnosis and usual route of spread?
A. A. Follicular carcinoma; haematogenous
B. B. Papillary carcinoma; lymphatic
C. C. Medullary carcinoma; haematogenous
D. D. Anaplastic carcinoma; local
These are the hallmarks of papillary carcinoma, which spreads mainly via lymphatics to the central and lateral neck nodes. Follicular carcinoma is characterised by capsular/vascular invasion and bloodborne spread.
Question 2 of 5A 30-year-old man has a thyroid mass, raised calcitonin and a family history of sudden death from hypertension. What must be done before thyroid surgery?
A. A. Radioactive iodine
B. B. Exclude phaeochromocytoma (metanephrines)
C. C. Start TSH suppression
D. D. Parathyroidectomy
Medullary cancer in MEN2 is associated with phaeochromocytoma. An unrecognised phaeo can cause a hypertensive crisis at induction. If present it is treated first, with alpha-blockade.
Question 3 of 5Which feature is true of medullary thyroid cancer?
A. A. It concentrates radioiodine
B. B. It is a tumour of follicular cells
C. C. It secretes thyroglobulin
D. D. It secretes calcitonin and is not iodine-avid
MTC arises from C cells, produces calcitonin and CEA, and does not take up iodine, so RAI is not used. Thyroglobulin is the marker for follicular-derived cancers.
Question 4 of 5A 78-year-old has a rapidly enlarging neck mass over 3 weeks with stridor. FNAC suggests anaplastic carcinoma. What is the priority?
A. A. Total thyroidectomy with neck dissection
B. B. Radioiodine ablation
C. C. Airway assessment and palliative multimodality planning
D. D. Observe
Anaplastic carcinoma is rapidly fatal and rarely resectable. Early airway assessment (tracheostomy/stent), staging, and MDT-led chemoradiotherapy or palliative care are the priorities.
Question 5 of 5Which feature, in a 1.5 cm papillary carcinoma, most strongly favours total thyroidectomy over lobectomy?
A. A. Female sex
B. B. Age 28
C. C. Multifocal bilateral disease or clinically involved lateral nodes
D. D. BMI above 30
Bilateral disease, nodal metastases, extrathyroidal extension, prior radiation and large size favour total thyroidectomy (± RAI). Age and sex alone do not.
